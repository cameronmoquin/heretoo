# Fun things HereToo can add instead of advertising

A working brainstorm. The platform makes its money two ways: the
$5/month family subscription, and a small set of paid gifts (welcome
cards, eventually printed Subject books). Everything else is free.

This document lists things to build that serve the same function
advertising serves on other platforms — *they make the platform a
place people return to* — without the cost ads carry.

Three notations after each item:
- **Build effort:** S (a Saturday), M (a long weekend), L (a real project).
- **Revenue / Delight:** R (sells), D (free, makes the room nicer), R+D (both).
- **Already shipped:** ✅ next to entries that exist today.

## Daily rhythms — micro-content seeded once a day

The mantel can hold one of these on top of the family's own postcards
— a shared platform-wide rhythm that all 12+ million grandmothers
encounter on the same day. Built from public-domain content, not
LLM-generated.

- **Word of the day** with one-paragraph etymology + read-aloud + a
  small *"this word reminds me of…"* prompt that posts to the family. (S, D)
- **Public-domain poem of the day** — Whitman, Dickinson, Hopkins,
  Rilke. Read-aloud cached as MP3. (S, D)
- **On this day in history** from public Wikipedia. One sentence,
  no rabbit-hole. (S, D)
- **Pattern of the week** — a single wallpaper from the museum library
  with a 100-word context card and a "wear it for the day" button. (S, D)
- **Recipe of the week** from public-domain cookbooks (Joy of Cooking
  pre-1929, Mrs. Beeton). One a week so families have time to make it. (M, D)
- **Letter of the day** from public-domain correspondence — Frida
  Kahlo, MLK, Dickens, Twain. The art of the reread, paired with a
  prompt: *"Whose letters live in your house?"* (M, D)
- **Constellation of the season** — naked-eye stars + the myth. Fall
  is the Pleiades. Spring is Leo. (S, D)
- **Birdcall of the morning** — public-domain Cornell Lab field
  recordings. Ten seconds, one species, the name. (S, D)
- **Quote of the day** curated, never AI-summarized. Editorial taste
  is the point. (S, D)

## Native games — built for the family graph

- **Two-truths-and-a-lie** — family edition. One member submits;
  others guess; the lie is revealed with the story behind it. (M, D)
- **"Who said this?"** auto-generated from a family's own posts.
  Multiple choice across family members. The grandkids will be
  brutal. (M, D)
- **Family memory match** — pairs of family photos with descriptions.
  Tile-flip game. Caches the family's collective face memory. (M, D)
- **Anagram on family names** — daily anagram puzzle whose source
  is the family roster. *"What 11-letter word is hidden in MARGARET
  KOWALSKI?"* (S, D)
- **Crossword from family vocabulary** — clues like *"What aunt Vee
  always called the cat (4)"*. Auto-generated weekly per family. (L, D)
- **Word-tower** — one player adds a word, next player adds one that
  shares a letter, family chain. (M, D)
- **Async dice / Yahtzee** — five dice, three rolls, family round
  that completes over a week. The grandfather can play in his own
  time. (M, D)
- **Pictionary prompt of the day** — daily drawing prompt; family
  members upload a sketch. Public-domain prompt list. (M, D)
- **Charades over video** — one-take 30-second video, others guess
  in chat. (M, D)

## Revenue alternatives to advertising

These are paid offerings the user opts into. The platform takes a
small margin; the rest goes to a vendor or a creative.

- **Welcome card printing** — $4 per printed card. (Specced.)
- **Subject books** — $80 per letterpress hardcover.
- **Family yearbook** — $45 per year-end book.
- **Photo restoration** — $12 per restored photo.
- **Custom wallpaper commissioning** — $200 to commission a working
  artist to design a pattern unique to your family. The platform
  curates the artists; the family owns the file forever. (R)
- **Voice clone subscription** — premium tier for the user's own
  voice to be used as their read-aloud narrator. Spec already calls
  for it. (R)
- **Memorial book** — auto-compiled, free for the family at print
  cost. Platform takes nothing. (R at break-even)
- **Pen-pal matching** — $5/year subscription for granddaughter +
  grandmother to be matched with another granddaughter +
  grandmother elsewhere. (R)
- **Recipe book printing** per family — $35. (R)
- **Birthday card pipeline** — the platform mails a printed card
  with a family-written message on a chosen birthday. $4 per card,
  same Lob pipeline as welcome cards. (R)
- **Family crest design service** — $120 for a working designer to
  hand-draw a family crest that becomes the family's avatar. (R)
- **A "Quiet Decade" subscription** — pay $500 once, the family is
  on the platform for ten years no questions asked. Covers grandma
  for the rest of her life. (R)

## What we still won't do

These belong on a refusal list and stay there:

- No banner ads, no sponsored posts.
- No data sale.
- No third-party SDK that surveils.
- No external-trivia content (NYT Mini, sports). If the user wants
  NYT Mini they have NYT Mini.
- No engagement-driven leaderboards above the family unit.
- No "AI grandma" or any product that resurrects a deceased relative.
- No "find your soulmate" features. The platform is for family.
- No paid-for placement in the parlor, ever.
- No streaks. No rank-up notifications. No daily-quest XP.

## Where to start

If we're picking one to ship next, the highest signal-per-effort
candidates are:

1. **Word of the day** with read-aloud + family discussion thread.
   Tiny build, instant return value, slots into the Room mantel as
   a fourth postcard. (S, D)
2. **Family yearbook** auto-compiled at year's end. $45 revenue,
   cements the platform's role in the family's literal archive. (L, R)
3. **The Family Bell** — one ring per day per family. The grandmother's
   first feature she shows off. (S, D)
4. **Voice notes with transcripts** — works for grandmothers
   (speak, don't type), works for ADHD adults (listen on the drive),
   works for hearing-impaired (read the transcript). Universal. (M, D)
5. **Daily check-in** — the *"I'm okay"* button. Quietly the most
   important feature on a family platform. (S, D)
