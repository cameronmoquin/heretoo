# HereToo — Positioning & Disruption Thesis

*How HereToo disrupts an entrenched social landscape. Written to sit
next to `heretoo-source-of-truth.md`. Plainspoken on purpose.*

---

## The two histories, and the lesson they share

**Myspace → Facebook.** Facebook didn't win by being a better Myspace.
Myspace was chaos — glitter, fake profiles, band spam, public by
default. Facebook started with a *bounded, real-identity graph* (one
campus, then colleges) where you only connected to people you actually
knew, and grew outward from trust. The disruption wasn't features. It
was **changing the unit of the network** — from "a page you decorate"
to "a real person in a real graph."

**The Instagram / Snapchat / TikTok fragmentation.** None of them beat
Facebook head-on either. Each annexed a *single behavior* Facebook
served badly: Instagram took aesthetic identity, Snapchat took
ephemeral close-friends intimacy, TikTok took algorithmic
entertainment. The lesson: **you never beat an incumbent on its own
turf. You win a behavior it structurally can't serve well.**

Put together: **you don't out-feature an incumbent — you change what
the network is made of, and what it's paid to do.**

---

## HereToo's two structural moves

### 1. Change the unit of the network

Myspace, Facebook, Instagram, Snap, TikTok — all share one atom: the
**individual profile**. HereToo's atom is the **family**. That isn't a
skin; it's a different graph.

- Joining a family wires you to everyone in it and their families
  (≤3 hops). The network has value from member #2, not member #10,000.
- One grandmother drags in fifteen people who would never have signed
  up for a "social network."
- It's a graph the incumbents can't reconstruct, because it's
  *invited, private, and kin-based* — not scraped from a contact list
  or an ad graph. **The graph is the moat, not the feature set.**

### 2. Change the incentive that runs it

This is the move competitors **cannot copy** — the innovator's dilemma
working in our favor.

- Meta and TikTok are advertising businesses. Their P&L *requires*
  maximizing attention, which means outrage, comparison, addiction.
- HereToo: art in the ad slot, $5/mo to remove ads, **the art stays
  either way**, no streaks, no nudges, "stays quiet when it should."
- That's not a feature gap — it's a position they can't follow us into
  without burning their core revenue. The durable wedge isn't something
  they clone in a sprint; it's an incentive they can't adopt.

Facebook changed the atom from page to person. HereToo changes it from
person to family, and the fuel from attention to subscription. **Two
moves they can't both answer.**

## Where to aim the wedge

- **The grandmother is the beachhead, not the teenager.** Everyone
  fights for the 14-year-old — the most contested, least loyal segment
  alive. The grandmother is the *underserved* one: she quit Facebook,
  hates the noise, and desperately wants to see the grandkids. If she
  adopts and pressures the family onto it (the source-of-truth's stated
  destination), we inherit the whole multi-generational graph for free.
  The teen-test still matters — the teen has to *tolerate* it — but
  grandma is the acquisition engine, not the teen.

- **Win the moment something is happening.** Routine life doesn't
  overcome switching inertia; crisis and transition do — a
  hospitalization (our origin story), a new baby, a deployment, a move,
  eldercare. Be the place families go *when something is happening*, and
  routine usage follows.

- **Distribution is the graph itself.** We don't have Meta's
  distribution and never will. But every invite is a *warm kinship
  referral* — the highest-converting channel that exists. Optimize
  relentlessly for "one tap to get grandma in" (the express invite-link
  signup already does this).

---

## The honest risks — because this isn't free

- **Tortoise, not hare.** The metric isn't DAU or engagement — chasing
  those would make us the thing we're disrupting. It's *families fully
  onboarded* and *retention of the multi-generational graph*. Slower,
  but stickier: families don't churn the way individuals do.

- **The real threat isn't Meta cloning it — it's apathy.** Nobody
  caring enough to switch for ordinary life. The crisis/transition wedge
  is the answer to that.

- **Stay disciplined about the calm.** The moment we bolt on a "for you"
  engagement feed to juice numbers, we've handed back our only
  structural advantage. Every feature still passes the seven-question
  filter, or it doesn't ship.
