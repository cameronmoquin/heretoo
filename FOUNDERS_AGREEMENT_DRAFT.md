## 1. The Company

The parties are forming **HereToo LLC** (the "Company"), a Limited Liability Company organized under the laws of the State of **[home state]**, to own, develop, and operate the HereToo family-first social platform operating at heretoo.social and on the iOS and Android app stores.

### 1a. Tax classification

The Company will initially be taxed as a **partnership** (the default for a multi-member LLC), which is a pass-through structure: profit and loss flow through to the Members' personal tax returns and are taxed only once.

When annual revenue exceeds **$50,000** or when the Members otherwise agree, the Company may elect **S-corporation tax treatment** (IRS Form 2553) to reduce self-employment tax on Cameron's compensation. The legal entity remains an LLC; only the tax classification changes.

The Company will not be a C-corporation. (If outside investment is ever sought from an institutional investor that requires C-corp structure, the Members will revisit at that time.)

### 1b. Lemonade-stand period

Until the Company is formally organized, the parties acknowledge that:

- HereToo is currently operated personally by Cameron under a "lemonade-stand" footing (no entity, no revenue).
- This Agreement constitutes a binding promise to issue equity per Sections 2 and 3 once the Company is organized.
- Each party has signed (or will sign before the Effective Date) the **Interim Intellectual Property Assignment** (separate document) transferring any HereToo-related work product to the Company-to-be-formed.

The Company will be formally organized on or before **the date of the first paying ad sale, OR [calendar date], whichever comes first**.

## 2. Equity Structure — Two Classes

The Company will issue two classes of membership interests:

### 2a. Class A — Voting

| Holder | Class A Interest |
|---|---|
| **Cameron Moquin** | **100% of Class A (60% of total economic interest)** |

Class A holders have **all voting rights** in the Company. All managerial decisions, strategic direction, hiring/firing, partnerships, contracts, and operational matters are made by the Class A Manager (Cameron). Class A is non-transferable except to a trust or estate of the holder, or by unanimous Member vote.

### 2b. Class B — Non-Voting Profit-Share

| Holder | Class B Interest |
|---|---|
| **[Brother 1]** | 50% of Class B (20% of total economic interest) |
| **[Brother 2]** | 50% of Class B (20% of total economic interest) |

Class B holders have **no voting rights** but share fully in **distributions of profit and proceeds of any sale** in proportion to their economic interest. Class B holders also have:

- Information rights (annual financial summary)
- Right to attend an annual Members' meeting
- ROFR + tag-along rights on any sale (Section 7)

### 2c. Why dual-class

Cameron is the operating founder, primary builder, and decision-maker. The brothers are participating as co-owners primarily to share in the financial upside of HereToo's success — to lift them out of unhoused status and into stable comfort. Dual-class structure honors this exactly: Cameron retains unambiguous operational control without having to negotiate every decision; the brothers receive the full economic benefit of ownership.

This is the same structure used by Google (GOOGL/GOOG), Meta, and Snap, scaled down to a family LLC.

### 2d. No option pool

No employee or advisor option pool is created at formation. If the Company later wishes to issue equity to an employee or advisor, it requires Cameron's sole written approval (Class A right). Doing so dilutes all existing holders proportionally. The brothers will be notified and may negotiate anti-dilution protection at that time.

## 3. Vesting

To protect against the risk that a Member becomes unreachable, hostile, or otherwise disengages early in the Company's life, all membership interests are **subject to a vesting schedule**:

- **Total vesting period:** 4 years
- **Cliff:** 1 year. Zero interest vests before the 1-year anniversary of the Effective Date. On the 1-year anniversary, **25%** of each Member's interest vests at once.
- **Monthly thereafter:** the remaining 75% vests in equal monthly installments over 36 months.

### 3a. Acceleration on acquisition

If the Company is acquired by a third party, **100%** of unvested interests accelerate to vested *if* the acquirer requires the acquired Member to depart within 12 months of close (double-trigger).

### 3b. Departure before fully vested

If a Member voluntarily withdraws, dies, becomes permanently disabled, is terminated for cause (3c), or otherwise stops being part of the Company before the end of the vesting period:

- **Vested interest** is retained by the departing Member or their estate.
- **Unvested interest** is forfeited and returned to the Company.
- The Company has a **right of first refusal** to repurchase vested Class B interests at fair market value if the holder later wishes to sell.

### 3c. "For cause" definition

A Member may be removed "for cause" by Cameron (Class A holder) for any of:

- Conviction of a felony or any crime involving moral turpitude
- Material breach of this Agreement that remains uncured 30 days after written notice
- Willful misconduct that materially harms the Company or its users
- Public conduct (including in HereToo content) that brings the Company into disrepute

Removal "without cause" requires repurchase of all vested interest at fair market value over a reasonable schedule (e.g., 5 annual installments).

## 4. Roles and Responsibilities

### 4a. Cameron — Manager and Class A Holder

Cameron has sole managerial authority. Responsibilities include:

- Product direction and technical architecture
- All hiring, contractor engagement, and termination decisions
- Fundraising (if any), banking, and financial management
- Legal and tax compliance
- Day-to-day execution

### 4b. Class B Holders — No Operational Obligations

[Brother 1] and [Brother 2] have **no operational obligation** to the Company. They are not expected to write code, manage users, or make business decisions. Their participation is in the form of profit-sharing only.

If a Class B holder *chooses* to contribute work (community management, family outreach, marketing copy, etc.), that contribution is voluntary and does not create additional equity beyond what is granted in Section 2.

## 6. Distributions and Compensation

### 6a. Compensation to Cameron

Cameron may, at his sole discretion as Manager, draw a reasonable salary or guaranteed payment from the Company once revenue supports it. This compensation is treated as an operating expense, not a distribution, and reduces the profit pool available for distribution.

### 6b. Profit distributions

If the Company generates net profit (after compensation and expenses) and Cameron determines it should be distributed rather than retained for operations, distributions are made **pro-rata to total economic interest**:

- 60% to Cameron
- 20% to [Brother 1]
- 20% to [Brother 2]

Distributions are at Cameron's discretion as Manager, but he commits to making distributions to the Class B holders **at least annually** if the Company has cumulative net profit and adequate operating reserves (defined as 6 months of operating expenses in the Company bank account).

### 6c. Tax distributions

When the Company has taxable income (which may or may not coincide with cash profit, given how partnerships are taxed), the Company will make a **mandatory tax distribution** sufficient to cover each Member's pro-rata share of estimated federal and state income tax on the allocated income, regardless of whether other distributions are made. This protects the brothers from being taxed on phantom income.

---

## 7. Right of First Refusal & Tag-Along

### 7a. Right of First Refusal (ROFR)

If any Class B holder wishes to sell, transfer, or assign their interest (in part or whole) to a third party, they must first offer it to the Company at fair market value with a 30-day acceptance window. If the Company declines, they may then offer to Cameron personally on the same terms before approaching any outside party.

### 7b. Drag-Along

If Cameron decides to sell the Company, all Members are obligated to sell on the same terms (this prevents a Class B holder from blocking a sale). Sale proceeds are distributed per Section 6b.

### 7c. Tag-Along

If Cameron sells any of his Class A interest to a third party (e.g., bringing in a partner), the Class B holders may **opt to** sell a proportional share of their interest on the same terms.

## 8. Confidentiality

Each Member agrees to keep confidential all non-public information about the Company, its strategy, its users, its finances, and its technology — both during their involvement and for **3 years** after departure.

**User content is sacred.** Family-circle posts and direct-message contents are inherently confidential to the users who created them and may not be accessed, viewed, or shared by Members except as necessary for technical support, legal compliance, or product debugging — and never for personal interest, gossip, or any non-Company purpose.

## 9. Non-Compete and Non-Solicit

For **12 months** after departure from the Company, no Member may:

- Build, operate, or contribute meaningfully to a competing family-first social product
- Solicit Company users, employees, or contractors for a competing venture
- Use Company trade secrets or confidential information

## 10. Dispute Resolution

We are family. Any dispute between Members will be resolved in this order:

1. **Direct conversation** — first.
2. **Mediation** through a mutually-agreed mediator. Costs split per economic interest (60/20/20).
3. If mediation fails after good-faith effort: **binding arbitration** under the rules of [JAMS / AAA] in [city, home state]. The arbitrator's decision is final and may be entered in any court of competent jurisdiction.

The Members agree to make a sincere effort to resolve disputes through conversation before invoking either mediation or arbitration. Family is the point.

## 11. Death, Disability, and Buy-Sell

If a Member dies or becomes permanently disabled:

- Vested interest transfers to their estate or designated beneficiary.
- Unvested interest is forfeited per Section 3b unless Cameron elects to accelerate it (e.g., out of compassion for the family).
- The Company has a **right to repurchase** the deceased/disabled Member's vested interest at fair market value over a reasonable schedule (e.g., 5 equal annual installments) so the operating Manager isn't suddenly co-owner with an outside party.

A separate **buy-sell agreement** with optional life insurance funding may be appropriate as the Company grows. Discuss with an attorney when revenue justifies it.
