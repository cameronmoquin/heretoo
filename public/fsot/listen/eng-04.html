Style, Register, and the Writing That Gets Graded

Transcript

Episode forty-eight: style and register — the judgment layer above grammar, where the test asks not is this correct but is this best. Warm-up; out loud. The number of applicants — singular or plural? Singular — the number is one; a number are many. Demands and recommendations take? The subjunctive — that it be. Here we go. First, register — matching formality to occasion, because the test loves to offer four grammatical sentences and ask which fits a formal document. The markers of formal register: no contractions; precise verbs over phrasal ones — investigate, not look into; tone even, claims measured. Drill: a cable reads, the minister's office totally stonewalled us on the pipeline thing. Formalize it. The minister's office declined repeated requests for information regarding the pipeline. Slang out, hedge gone, the noun named. But formality is not inflation — the opposite trap is bureaucratic fog: it has been determined that a suboptimal outcome eventuated. Deflate it. We failed — or, kindlier, the effort did not succeed. Formal means precise and impersonal where personality distracts; it never means longer. When two options are equally formal, the test wants the shorter. Second: tone control — hedges and intensifiers, the throttle of claims. Overclaiming: this proves the ministry is corrupt. Under a single anecdote's evidence, calibrate it. This suggests, or is consistent with — remember the logic episodes: the verb must match the strength of the inference. Overhedging: it would seem that perhaps there may be some possibility of delay. A delay is possible — one hedge, chosen, is a judgment; four hedges is a shrug. The reporting officer's art, and the test's: one calibrated verb — confirms, suggests, alleges, denies — carrying exactly the evidence's weight. Alleges versus says versus confirms is a scale, not synonyms. Third: emphasis by architecture. End position is stressed position — the sentence's final slot is its spotlight. Compare: The program failed, despite years of funding. Versus: Despite years of funding, the program failed. Which lands the failure? The second — failed owns the spotlight. Put the payload last; spend the openings on the known, arrive at the new. Same logic at paragraph scale — remember episode twenty-nine: the opener orients, the closer lands. Periodic sentences bank the meaning until the end and hit hardest; loose sentences front-load and relax. Neither is wrong; the test asks which arrangement emphasizes the intended element — find the element, check what sits last. Fourth: coordination versus subordination — grammar as a theory of importance. And says the two facts are peers; although, because, while demote one to background. The minister resigned, and the markets fell — two headlines. After the minister resigned, the markets fell — one headline, one context, plus a whiff of causation. Drill: combine these to emphasize the markets — the treaty stalled; investors withdrew forty billion. While the treaty stalled, investors withdrew forty billion dollars — the stall subordinated, the money in the spotlight. The choice-of-conjunction questions are importance questions in disguise; ask what deserves the main clause. Fifth: the diplomat's special register — constructive ambiguity and its uses, because State's own tradition is tested judgment. Agreed language survives because each side reads it its way — acknowledges differences over the Taiwan question; serious consequences; land for peace itself, remember 242, deliberately silent on how much land. In drafting, precision is the default and ambiguity a tool used on purpose — never by accident. The sin is unintended ambiguity: the ambassador discussed the crisis with the minister before she briefed the press — who briefed? Ambiguous pronoun across two possible antecedents — name her: before the ambassador briefed the press. Every she, this, and the latter must survive a hostile reading; diplomatic texts get exactly that reading, forever. The synthesis drill — one bad paragraph, rebuilt aloud, every tool at once. The draft: It would seem that there may be issues with the visa section. Wait times have really exploded and it's a total mess. Each and every applicant we talked to complained about them. Steps are being taken by management at this point in time. Rebuild it — register, calibration, architecture, and one honest agent. One version: Visa wait times have tripled since March. Every applicant interviewed cited the delays. Management is now reassigning staff to the section. Claims sized to evidence, fog burned off, the actor named, the fix in the spotlight — four sentences to three, nothing lost but the noise. Recall block. Formal register means? Precise and unpadded — never merely longer. The hedging rule? One calibrated hedge is judgment; a pile is a shrug. The spotlight sits where? Sentence-final — payload last. Subordination decides? What's headline and what's background — the main clause is the claim. Constructive ambiguity is? Ambiguity on purpose — the sin is the accidental kind. That closes the English block, and with it the drills. One episode remains: the world since the bank's horizon — current affairs, refreshed as the test approaches. Thanks for listening.
