OMST episode six, the last in this track: English expression, and how to spend the final week. Review first; out loud. What generates the tempting wrong answer in a hard situational case? A real virtue: loyalty, helpfulness, deference, discretion, diligence. The six-step situational method, first two steps? Who owns the decision; what category is this. What is the standard for an inference? Must be true. Three questions. One: what does English expression test on the OMST, and which episodes of this course already cover it? Two: what should the last week look like? Three: what happens in the room, and how do you spend the clock? Here we go. Question one. English expression is the section that is genuinely shared between the OMST and the officer test, so the work is already done and sitting in this course. Episode twenty-eight, Grammar That Scores, drills the rules by ear: subject-verb agreement across intervening phrases, pronoun case, dangling and misplaced modifiers, parallelism, comma splices, and the confusable pairs. Episode twenty-nine, Paragraph Architecture, handles sentence ordering, transitions, and concision. Episode forty-seven is a twenty-item gauntlet, and forty-eight covers register and style, which matters more for you than for an officer candidate: an Office Management Specialist proofs and edits documents going out over someone else's name, so the test leans on exactly that judgment. Work those four, plus the English exam in the practice app. That is the whole English preparation, and it is the section where a point is cheapest to gain. Two reminders from that material, because they show up constantly in workplace prose. First, the subject is never inside a prepositional phrase: the report on visa backlogs and staffing shortages was submitted, singular, no matter how many plural nouns sit between the subject and the verb. Second, an opening modifier attaches to the very next noun, so having reviewed the cable, the decision was easy is wrong, because decisions do not review cables. If you hear nothing else in the final week, hear those two. Question two: the final week, and the shape of it matters because you get one attempt in twelve months. Do not learn new material in the last week. Everything from here is consolidation and rehearsal. Spend the week on three things: timed sections under real conditions, review of what you got wrong, and sleep. Timed practice first. Use the practice app's OMST exams, which drill only the three real sections: the full mixed exam, and the separate situational judgment and reading comprehension papers. The point of timing is not the score, it is calibration, learning what a minute and a half feels like so the clock stops being a surprise. Take at least one full-length sitting at roughly the hour of day you will actually test, which for you is late afternoon. Then review, and review properly, which means reviewing the wrong answers, not the right ones. For every item you missed, say out loud which of the two failures it was: did you not know the rule, or did you know it and misread the stem. Those need different fixes. Not knowing is studying. Misreading is slowing down on the first line of the question, and it is the more common of the two. And spacing beats cramming, which is not a motivational slogan but the most replicated finding in the study of memory. Four half-hour sessions across four days beat one four-hour session the night before, by a wide margin, for material you need to still have a week later. The night before, do very little: one light pass over the situational method and the reading traps, then stop. Midpoint check. What are the two reasons you miss an item, and what is the fix for each? Not knowing the rule, which is studying; and misreading the stem, which is slowing down at the first line. Question three: the room. You will test at a Pearson VUE center, so the practical things are the boring things and they are worth handling in advance. Know where the center is and how long the trip takes at that hour; a five o'clock appointment means arriving in afternoon traffic. Bring the identification the confirmation specifies, exactly as it specifies. Expect to store your belongings, expect a check-in process, and expect to arrive early. None of this is difficult, and all of it is easier to do the week before than the afternoon of. How to spend the clock, section by section. On English expression, move fast: these items are short and your ear either catches the error or it does not, so a long stare rarely converts a wrong answer into a right one. Bank the time here. On reading comprehension, spend your time in the passage, not in the options. One careful read for structure, main idea in your own words, then targeted re-reads for each question. Do not answer from memory. If two options survive elimination, take the more modest one and move on. On situational judgment, read the stem twice, always, because first step and best course of action are different questions and that one word decides more items than anything else on the section. Then run the method: who owns the decision, what category, cut the rule-breakers, cut the passive, prefer act-and-inform, prefer sooner. Do not overthink these. Your considered second guess on a judgment item is usually a rationalization, and the answer that protects the principal's time, the accuracy of the record, and the security of the post is the answer the job would give. A word on guessing: answer everything. Unanswered is wrong with certainty; a guess after eliminating even one option is better than that, and there is no penalty for being wrong that you do not already incur by leaving it blank. If you are running out of time in a section, spend the last minute filling in rather than perfecting. Final recall, everything in this track. The three sections of the OMST? English expression, reading comprehension, situational judgment. The two questions every situational item is really asking? Who owns this decision, and how fast does it need to move. The five archetypes? Bulldozer, ostrich, escalator, people-pleaser, deflector. The four reading traps? Overstatement, outside fact, detail promoted, reversal. The three things the role protects? The principal's time, the accuracy of the record, the security of the post. That is the track. You have a method for the section nobody practices, a mechanical procedure for the section that punishes cleverness, and the English work was already done. One attempt, so finish the preparation before you walk in. Good luck on the nineteenth. Thanks for listening.
