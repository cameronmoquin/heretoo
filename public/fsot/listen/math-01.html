Episode twenty-six: mental math — the toolkit, drilled out loud. The at-home test gives you a text box, not a calculator, so the skill is estimation and elimination: get to one significant figure fast, kill three answer choices, compute exactly only when two survive. This episode is a drill — I give the technique, then reps; answer in the pause before I do. Review first. China's demographic phrase? Getting old before getting rich. Remittances versus aid? Several times larger. Good. Numbers now. Tool one: percents by decomposition. Ten percent is a decimal shift; five percent is half of that; one percent is two shifts. Build anything from those bricks. Fifteen percent of two hundred forty? Ten percent is twenty-four, five percent is twelve — thirty-six. Seven percent of three hundred? One percent is three; seven of them — twenty-one. And the flip trick, because A percent of B equals B percent of A. Eight percent of twenty-five? Flip it: twenty-five percent of eight — two. Four percent of seventy-five? Seventy-five percent of four — three. Tool two: percent change — always divide the change by the starting number, and know that the direction matters. From eighty up to one hundred? Twenty over eighty — twenty-five percent up. From one hundred down to eighty? Twenty over a hundred — twenty percent down. Same twenty, different base: up twenty-five, down twenty. That asymmetry is a favorite trap. Sequential changes never cancel: down fifty percent then up fifty percent lands where? Seventy-five — half of a hundred is fifty, up fifty percent of fifty is seventy-five. Up twenty then down twenty? Ninety-six — one point two times point eight. Multiply the factors; never add the percents. Tool three: percentage points versus percent — when both phrasings appear in the options, that distinction is the whole question. A rate rises from four percent to five percent. In points? One point. As a percent increase? Twenty-five percent — one over four. If an option says one percent and another says twenty-five percent, you now know which is which. Tool four: fractions as reflexes. The eighths: one eighth is twelve and a half, then twenty-five, thirty-seven and a half, fifty, sixty-two and a half, seventy-five, eighty-seven and a half. The sixths: sixteen point seven, thirty-three, fifty, sixty-seven, eighty-three. Three eighths of four hundred? One hundred fifty. Five sixths of three hundred? Two hundred fifty. A three-to-five split means what share for the smaller side? Three of eight parts — thirty-seven and a half percent. Ratios always sum their parts first. Tool five: growth and doubling — the rule of seventy: seventy divided by the growth rate gives the doubling time. Two percent growth doubles in? About thirty-five years. Seven percent? Ten years. An economy growing seven percent for twenty years grows how much? Two doublings — four times. Compounding for a few years at small rates: add the rates, then add a little. Three years at five percent is a bit over fifteen — fifteen point eight. And two deflators you know from economics: real equals nominal minus inflation — nominal raise six, inflation four, real raise? Two percent. Per capita growth equals GDP growth minus population growth — GDP up four, population up one? Three percent per capita. Tool six: division hygiene. Strip matching zeros: three hundred sixty thousand divided by twelve hundred? Three hundred sixty over one point two — three hundred. Reverse percents: X is two percent of what — multiply X by fifty. Six is two percent of? Three hundred. Twelve is four percent of? Three hundred — twelve times twenty-five. The reverse-percent family: two percent means times fifty, four percent times twenty-five, five percent times twenty, ten percent times ten. Tool seven: weighted averages lean toward the bigger group — never split the difference. A post has thirty officers averaging age forty and ten local staff averaging sixty. Average age overall? Not fifty — weight three to one: forty-five. Three parts forty, one part sixty. A country's exports are eighty percent oil growing zero percent and twenty percent tech growing ten percent. Total export growth? Two percent — the big sluggish part drags the average toward itself. The gauntlet — six fast ones, mixed. Twelve and a half percent of six hundred forty? Eighty — one eighth. A budget rises from two hundred to two hundred fifty million — percent change? Up twenty-five percent. Down forty percent then up fifty percent from a hundred? Ninety — point six times one point five. Unemployment falls from eight percent to six: points and percent? Two points; twenty-five percent decline. Growth at three point five percent doubles in about? Twenty years — seventy over three point five. Aid splits seven to three; the smaller share of two hundred million? Sixty million — three tenths. That's the toolkit: decompose percents, divide by the start, multiply the factors, know your eighths, seventy for doubling, strip zeros, lean with the weights. Replay at speed until none of them require thought. Next: statistics — the honest and dishonest uses of numbers. Thanks for listening.
