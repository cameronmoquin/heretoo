Deep Dive, Depression and World War

US History · 8:19 · Episode 34

Open the full player

Transcript

Deep dive five: the Depression anatomized, the New Deal program by program, and the war economy that ended both. Warm-up; out loud. Rockefeller's method versus Carnegie's? Horizontal — buy the rivals; vertical — own the chain. The Populist platform's afterlife? Income tax, direct election, elastic currency — enacted by others. The Platt Amendment leashed? Cuba. Three questions. One: what actually caused the Depression — the mechanism, not just the Crash? Two: which New Deal programs did what — and which ones survive today? Three: how did the war remake the home front — women, Black Americans, science, and the state? Here we go. Question one: the Crash of October 1929 was the trigger, not the cause; stocks bought on ten percent margin unwound in a cascade of margin calls — Black Thursday, Black Tuesday. But crashes had happened before without decade-long depressions. The mechanism came in four gears grinding together. Gear one: banking collapse — nine thousand banks failed by 1933, no deposit insurance, so every rumor emptied a bank; failures destroyed money itself, a third of the money supply gone, and the Fed — Friedman's indictment from the economics episode — let it happen, even raising rates to defend gold. Gear two: demand collapse — the paradox of thrift live: frightened consumers stopped buying, firms stopped investing, each cut deepening the next; undergirded by the twenties' weak spots — farm depression all decade, top-heavy incomes, installment debt. Gear three: the gold standard as transmission belt — countries defending gold parities with tight money exported deflation to each other; those that left gold early, like Britain in 1931, recovered first — a test-grade correlation. Gear four: Smoot-Hawley and retaliation strangling trade. Hoover was no do-nothing — the Reconstruction Finance Corporation lent to banks and railroads — but he vetoed direct relief and preached balanced budgets, and the Bonus Army's rout in 1932 — veterans tear-gassed off the Mall — fixed his image. Unemployment: twenty-five percent; steel ran at twelve percent capacity; the Dust Bowl blew Oklahoma onto the road to California — Grapes of Wrath country. Question two: the New Deal, sorted by the three R's, with survivors flagged. Relief, immediate: the bank holiday and Emergency Banking Act stopped the runs in a week — Roosevelt's first fireside chat talking the country into redepositing; the CCC put three million young men into forests — roads, trails, trees; the WPA under Hopkins built airports, schools, and post offices and hired writers, painters, and photographers — Dorothea Lange's migrant mother; direct relief through FERA. Recovery, the experiments: the NRA's blue eagle codes — struck down in Schechter, largely unmourned; the AAA paying farmers to cut production — struck down too, recreated with new footing, ancestor of every farm bill since; the TVA, government power and flood control across seven states — surviving, the era's boldest institution; and going off gold domestically in 1933, devaluing to reflate — the gold-standard gear thrown into reverse. Reform, the survivors: the FDIC — deposit insurance, no major runs since; the SEC policing Wall Street, Joseph Kennedy its first chairman; Glass-Steagall separating banking from speculation — repealed 1999, name-checked ever since; the Wagner Act's National Labor Relations Board — union membership tripling, the CIO organizing whole industries, sit-down strikes taking General Motors in 1937; the Fair Labor Standards Act, 1938 — minimum wage, forty-hour week, child labor banned; and Social Security, 1935 — old-age pensions, unemployment insurance, aid to dependent children: the third rail, still humming. The coalition it built — labor, cities, immigrants, farmers, and Black voters leaving Lincoln's party — realigned politics for two generations. The limits: agricultural and domestic workers excluded from Social Security's start — most Black workers, by design of Southern committee chairs; the recession of 1937 when FDR cut spending too soon — the Keynesian cautionary tale; and recovery incomplete until the war. Midpoint check. Which left first and recovered first — gold-standard logic? Countries that abandoned gold early, like Britain, reflated and recovered first; the standard transmitted deflation. Question three: the war remade everything it touched. Production: the War Production Board converted Detroit — a bomber an hour at Willow Run; GDP doubled; unemployment vanished to one percent; the Depression ended not by theory but by the largest Keynesian stimulus imaginable, deficits near thirty percent of GDP. Paying for it: war bonds, rationing — meat, gas, rubber — price controls under the OPA, and mass income taxation with paycheck withholding invented in 1943: the modern tax state, born of war and never repealed. Women: six million into war industries — Rosie the Riveter — plus the WACs and WAVEs; most were pushed out in 1945, but the precedent stayed. Black Americans: a million served, segregated — the Tuskegee Airmen's escort record; A. Philip Randolph's threatened march on Washington extracted Executive Order 8802 banning defense-industry discrimination — the first federal civil rights order since Reconstruction; the Double V campaign — victory over fascism abroad and Jim Crow at home; and the Great Migration's second wave moved millions north and west to war plants, remaking every city it touched. The shame you know — internment and Korematsu; add that Japanese American volunteers from the camps formed the 442nd, the most decorated unit of its size in Army history. Science: the Manhattan Project — Oppenheimer at Los Alamos, two billion dollars, the government-university-industry triangle that became the postwar research state, radar and penicillin scaled alongside. And the veterans' dividend: the GI Bill, 1944 — college, home loans, business capital for sixteen million veterans — building the suburban middle class, though its benefits ran through segregated institutions and redlined mortgages, so the dividend compounded unequally. War's end you know: Truman, the bomb, and only two powers left standing. Recall block. The Depression's four gears? Bank collapse killing money; demand spiral; gold-standard transmission; trade war. Three New Deal survivors, rapid fire? FDIC, SEC, Social Security — add the NLRB and the minimum wage. The 1937 recession teaches? Austerity too soon re-collapses demand — Keynes's cautionary chapter. Executive Order 8802 did what, extracted by whom? Banned defense-industry discrimination; Randolph's threatened march. Withholding arrived when and why? 1943 — mass taxation to fund the war; the modern tax state. Next deep dive: Cold War America at home — McCarthy to Reagan, and the movement that finished Reconstruction's docket. Thanks for listening.
