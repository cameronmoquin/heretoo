Regional deep dive one: East Asia — the history under the headlines of episode nineteen. Warm-up; out loud. The four acids on the New Deal coalition? Vietnam, race, disorder, the seventies economy. Strategic ambiguity governs which strait? Taiwan's. China's Malacca dilemma? Its energy transits a strait the US Navy could close. Three questions. One: what is the century of humiliation, and why does it explain Chinese policy today? Two: how did Japan rise, fall, and rise reformed? Three: why did the little dragons boom, and what did their model prove? Here we go. Question one: China's own frame for its history is the century of humiliation, 1839 to 1949 — and whatever you think of the framing, Beijing governs by it, so learn it. The sequence: the Opium Wars — Britain forcing the drug trade at gunpoint, Hong Kong taken, the treaty ports opened, extraterritoriality exempting foreigners from Chinese law; the Taiping catastrophe; the Sino-Japanese War of 1895 — Taiwan ceded to Japan, the first humiliation by an Asian power; the Boxer Rebellion crushed by eight foreign armies occupying Beijing; the dynasty's fall in 1911; the warlord chaos; and Japan's invasion — Manchuria 1931, the full war from 1937, the Rape of Nanjing's mass atrocity. The Communist victory in 1949 is, in the official telling, the moment China stood up. So when Beijing speaks of national rejuvenation by 2049 — the centenary — of unification with Taiwan as sacred, of never again accepting foreign dictates, it is running that script. Mao's own decades add the internal catastrophes: the Great Leap Forward's famine, 1958 to 62, tens of millions dead — the deadliest famine in history; the Cultural Revolution, 1966 to 76, a decade of ideological terror. Then the pivot that made the present: Deng Xiaoping, from 1978 — reform and opening: special economic zones, markets under party control, to get rich is glorious — and the bargain sealed at Tiananmen in 1989: prosperity, not politics; the party delivered the greatest poverty reduction in history — eight hundred million — and kept every lever. Xi Jinping's turn since 2012 tightened the party's grip, ended term limits, and swapped Deng's hide your strength for wolf-warrior assertion. The through-line for the test: Chinese foreign policy is domestic legitimacy projected outward — humiliation reversed, rejuvenation promised. Question two: Japan's arc is the mirror — the Asian power that joined the imperial club, burned itself down, and rebuilt as its opposite. The Meiji ascent you know from the spine; add the machinery: state-sponsored industry sold to family conglomerates — the zaibatsu — a Prussian-style constitution, and universal education. Empire followed the Western template: Taiwan 1895, Korea annexed 1910 — a brutal colonization Koreans have not forgotten, the comfort women issue still live in Tokyo-Seoul relations; Manchuria's puppet state; the China war; and the Pacific gamble of 1941 — resource embargoes answered by Pearl Harbor, the empire of the Greater East Asia Co-Prosperity Sphere at bayonet point. The fall: firebombed, atom-bombed, occupied. The reformation is the exam-worthy part: MacArthur's occupation wrote the 1947 constitution — women's suffrage, land reform breaking the estates, unions legalized, and Article Nine renouncing war, the famous peace clause; the emperor kept as symbol, war crimes tried in Tokyo. Then the economic miracle: Korea War procurement as the spark, MITI's industrial policy, the keiretsu networks, lifetime employment, and export excellence — by the 1980s Japan was the world's second economy and Americans feared Toyota the way they now discuss China. The bubble burst in 1990 — three lost decades of deflation, remember the economics episode — and today's Japan matters differently: the anchor of the American alliance system, host to the largest forward-deployed forces, quietly shedding Article Nine's limits — defense spending doubling, counterstrike missiles — because of China's rise and Korea's missiles. The pacifist rearming: that's the current-events sentence. Midpoint check. Why does 1949 function as sacred time in Beijing's telling? It closes the century of humiliation — the party's legitimacy rests on being the force that ended it and the guarantor it never returns. Question three: the little dragons — South Korea, Taiwan, Singapore, Hong Kong — plus the Southeast Asian tier behind them. The boom recipe, common across them: land reform, export-led manufacturing — start with textiles, climb to chips — high savings, ferocious education, state-guided credit, and American security plus market access as the platform. The proof they offered, test-relevant: development is possible in a generation — South Korea moved from Ghana's income level in 1960 to the rich world, the strongest counterexample to geographic or cultural determinism. The political kicker: several democratized as they enriched — Korea's 1987 transition from military rule, Taiwan's from martial law to full democracy by the 1990s, now the counter-model to Beijing's bargain and part of why the strait matters morally as well as strategically. Southeast Asia: ASEAN's ten, consensus-bound and neutral by design, hedging between Washington and Beijing; Vietnam — Communist, capitalist, and America-friendly out of China anxiety, the era's oddest couple; Indonesia — fourth most populous, largest Muslim-majority country, the region's quiet giant; the Philippines — the American treaty ally with reefs actually contested, its coast guard water-cannoned in the South China Sea. And the Koreas complete the board from episode twenty: one peninsula, two proofs — the same people, divided by system, produced the world's most striking natural experiment: satellite photos of the dark North beside the blazing South are the test's favorite development image. Recall block. The century of humiliation runs? 1839 to 1949 — Opium Wars to the People's Republic. Deng's bargain, and Xi's turn? Prosperity for obedience; Xi tightened the party and dropped the hiding. Article Nine is? Japan's war-renouncing clause — now being outgrown in practice. The dragons proved? Poverty to riches in a generation is possible — export-led, education-heavy, state-guided. The peninsula's natural experiment shows? Institutions, not geography or culture — one people, two systems, night and day. Next region: the Middle East from the Ottomans forward. Thanks for listening.
