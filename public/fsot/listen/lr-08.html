Logic Bootcamp, The Final Gauntlet

Logical Reasoning · 5:31 · Episode 44

Transcript

Logic bootcamp four: the final gauntlet — twenty items, test pace, everything mixed: flaws, inferences, assumptions, strengthen-weaken, conclusions. Short beat, answer aloud, my answer follows. This is the last drill; leave nothing in the tank. Go. One. Sanctions preceded the recession, so sanctions caused it. Post hoc. Two. All attachés attended; some attendees were late; so some attachés were late. Doesn't follow — the latecomers may all be non-attachés; circles, not overlap. Three. The plan will work because failure is not an option. Circular — determination restated as evidence. Four. Two hundred of our four hundred officers speak Spanish; two hundred fifty speak French. Minimum bilinguals? Fifty — add, subtract the total. Five. If the vote passes, the treaty ratifies. The treaty did not ratify. The vote did not pass — modus tollens, valid. Six. Embassies with gardens report higher morale; plant gardens to raise morale. Selection and confound — pleasant posts have both; no causal license. Seven. The whistleblower is a disgruntled ex-employee, so her claims are false. Ad hominem — motive invites scrutiny, never verdicts. Eight. Either we fund the program fully or we cancel it. False dilemma — partial funding exists. Nine. The audit found errors in three percent of vouchers. What's needed before judging that number? A benchmark — three percent against what norm, what error severity? Numbers need companions. Ten. Only citizens may vote; Dana voted. Dana is a citizen — the only grants it. Eleven. Citizens may vote; Dana is a citizen; so Dana voted. Doesn't follow — may is permission, not action. Twelve. Our democracy programs succeeded: five partner countries held elections. Missing counterfactual and baseline — would they have voted anyway? Elections held is also a weak measure of democracy. Thirteen. Every proposal from that bureau has been overbudget, so this one will be too. Induction — probable, never compelled; as an inference it fails, as a bet it's reasonable. Know which question is being asked. Fourteen. The conclusion of: visa fraud rose; staffing fell; therefore the section needs more officers. Needs more officers — the therefore claim; the rest is evidence. And the assumption: staffing, not process or demand, drives the fraud trend. Fifteen. No unclassified system may store this cable. This system stores it. The system is classified — contrapositive through the no. Sixteen. The pilot program cut wait times in the three posts that volunteered for it. Roll it out worldwide. Selection again — volunteers differ; add external validity: three posts to the world is a long transfer. Seventeen. Strengthen: cameras cut warehouse theft — which helps most: A, thefts fell the month cameras arrived and stayed low; B, employees say they like the cameras? A — time-locked change; B is filler that fits every story. Eighteen. Since no delegation objected during the session, all delegations support the resolution. Silence read as consent — an appeal-to-ignorance cousin; abstention, tactics, and sleeping delegates all fit the same silence. Nineteen. Our language institute's graduates score higher than self-study officers, so institute instruction is superior. Selection at intake — who gets sent to the institute? Adjust before comparing. Twenty, the closer. The department should expand the fellowship, because it attracts talented officers, and talented officers improve the service. Valid or flawed? Valid in form — premises to conclusion — but test the load-bearing assumption: attracts means causes to join who otherwise wouldn't; if the talented would join regardless, the fellowship buys nothing. Counterfactual, one last time. That's the gauntlet, and the bootcamps entire. The section rewards exactly four habits: name the question before the argument; hold inferences to must; hunt the assumption with negation; and judge causes against rivals and counterfactuals. Those habits are now yours — twenty at a time, out loud, at speed. Next: development economics — why nations escape poverty, and why some don't. Thanks for listening.
