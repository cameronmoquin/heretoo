Regional deep dive three: Europe and Russia — the union built to end war, and the empire that keeps returning. Warm-up; out loud. Sèvres and Lausanne bracket whose denial? The Kurds'. 1979's triple? Iran, Mecca, Afghanistan. The 2003 lesson? Regime removal without a state plan makes a vacuum. Three questions. One: what is the EU actually — the machine, piece by piece? Two: what happened to the lands between Germany and Russia after 1989? Three: what is Russia's recurring pattern, tsar to Putin? Here we go. Question one: the European Union began as a war-prevention device with a coal-and-steel disguise: the Schuman Plan of 1950 pooled French and German war industries precisely so the two could never again arm against each other — integration as peace strategy, the founders Monnet and Schuman betting that merged economies make merged interests. The ladder since: the Treaty of Rome, 1957, six countries, a common market; enlargements taking in Britain, the Mediterranean's ex-dictatorships — anchoring Spain, Portugal, and Greece's young democracies, the club as democratic stabilizer — then Maastricht, 1992, creating the Union and the euro's blueprint; the big bang of 2004, ten new members, mostly the freed East; twenty-seven members today. The machine: the Commission in Brussels proposes law and polices the single market — the unelected executive the tabloids resent; the Council of member governments and the directly elected Parliament co-decide; the European Court of Justice makes EU law supreme over national law — the federal feature; the European Central Bank in Frankfurt runs the euro for twenty members, remember Greece and the trilemma; and Schengen abolishes internal borders for most of the continent. The test's distinction: the single market's four freedoms — goods, services, capital, persons — versus the intergovernmental fields, foreign policy and defense, where any capital can veto: economic giant, political confederation. The stress tests, rapid fire: the eurozone crisis — one currency, no common treasury, austerity's resentments; the 2015 migration wave splitting East from West over quotas; Brexit — the 2016 referendum, fifty-two to forty-eight, the first exit, trading full access for full sovereignty, with the Northern Ireland border its hardest knot; and the rule-of-law fights with Hungary's Orbán — illiberal democracy inside the club, funds withheld as discipline. Then Ukraine, which answered the drift question: the war produced common weapons purchases, Russian-energy divorce, and candidacies for Ukraine and Moldova — crisis, as usual, integrating what treaties couldn't. Question two: the lands between. Nineteen eighty-nine freed the Warsaw Pact tier — Poland's Solidarity had led since 1980, Gdansk's electrician Wałęsa; Havel's Velvet Revolution took Czechoslovakia, which then split velvetly in two; only Romania's exit shed blood. The formula, dual accession: NATO for safety, EU for wealth — Poland, the Baltics, and the rest joined both within fifteen years; Poland became Europe's growth star and, after 2022, its rising military heavyweight — the eastern flank's anchor. Yugoslavia took the other road: Tito's federation of six republics, held by his prestige and non-alignment — remember the Non-Aligned Movement — dissolved in blood as Milošević rode Serb nationalism: Croatia's war, then Bosnia, 1992 to 95 — siege of Sarajevo, ethnic cleansing entering the vocabulary, Srebrenica's genocide of eight thousand under a UN safe area's flag — ended only by NATO bombs and the Dayton Accords, an American-brokered constitution that froze the war into a state; then Kosovo, 1999 — NATO's air war without UN blessing, humanitarian intervention's precedent, independence declared in 2008 and still unrecognized by Serbia, Russia, and five EU members. The Balkan file today: candidates queuing, Serbia between Brussels and Moscow, Bosnia gridlocked — Europe's unfinished basement. Midpoint check. Why did integration follow crisis so often? Because sovereignty concedes only under pressure — coal and steel after war, the euro after reunification, joint debt after COVID, defense after Ukraine: each emergency buys what argument couldn't. Question three: Russia's pattern, four beats, repeating since the tsars: expansion for buffer, modernization from above, brittle autocracy, collapse — and again. Muscovy grew by absorbing the lands between it and every invader — Mongols, Poles, Swedes, Napoleon, Hitler — until the buffer was an empire: eleven time zones, security through depth, the strategic culture that reads neighbors' independence as threat. Modernization from above: Peter the Great building St. Petersburg on serf bones, Catherine's enlightened veneer, Stalin's five-year plans — the state forcing modernity without sharing power; the pattern's cost, chronic: institutions stay personal, succession stays crisis. Collapse and return: 1917 you know; 1991 next — and the nineties are the key to Putin: hyperinflation eating savings, GDP halving, oligarchs looting privatization, NATO enlarging, and the state humiliated — the decade Russians remember as chaos and the West remembers as freedom. Putin's restoration ran the old script: energy wealth recentralized, television captured, oligarchs subordinated, elections managed — sovereign democracy — and the buffer reasserted: Chechnya razed, Georgia 2008, Crimea 2014, Ukraine 2022, each testing and finding the West's response tolerable until the last. The war's Russia: economy militarized and sanctions-adapted — remember the fever chart from episode nineteen — dissent criminalized, Navalny dead in an Arctic prison, and dependence on China deepening into junior partnership: the empire returned, at the price of becoming a resource appendage to Beijing. The open question you carry from episode nineteen stands: whether the pattern's next beat — brittleness — arrives, and what collapses or hardens after Putin. Recall block. The Schuman logic? Pool the war industries so war becomes impossible — integration as peace. The four freedoms? Goods, services, capital, persons. Dayton did what? Froze Bosnia's war into a constitution — peace as permanent gridlock. The dual accession formula? NATO for safety, EU for wealth. Russia's four-beat pattern? Expand for buffer, modernize from above, harden, collapse — repeat. Next region: Africa — fifty-four states, three inheritances, and the youngest continent's century. Thanks for listening.
