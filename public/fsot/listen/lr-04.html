Logic Drill 4, The Gauntlet

Logical Reasoning · 6:34 · Episode 18

Transcript

Logic drill four: the gauntlet. No new material today — this is the speed round. Fifteen short arguments; after each, name the flaw or the move out loud before I do. The point is automaticity: on test day you get about two minutes a question, and naming the pattern in three seconds buys you the other hundred seventeen for the answer choices. The full vocabulary is in play: post hoc, hasty generalization, composition, division, false dilemma, appeal to ignorance, ad hominem, circular reasoning, affirming the consequent, denying the antecedent, selection effect, missing denominator, missing baseline, necessary versus sufficient, and irrelevant criticism. Deep breath. Go. One. Since the new ambassador arrived, tourism has doubled. She's clearly good for business. Post hoc — after her arrival isn't because of it. Two. Three visa applicants lied to me this morning. Applicants from this country simply cannot be trusted. Hasty generalization — three cases, one morning. Three. Every country in the alliance is militarily weak. So the alliance is militarily weak. Composition — weak parts can still mass into a strong whole; that's the point of alliances. Four. The committee is deadlocked. So every member of it must be undecided. Division — a fifty-fifty split of very decided people deadlocks fine. Five. If the summit succeeds, both leaders will attend the ceremony. Both leaders attended. The summit must have succeeded. Affirming the consequent — they might attend a funeral for the deal, too. Six. Only fluent speakers pass the language exam. Kim is a fluent speaker. So Kim will pass. Necessary versus sufficient — fluency is required, not enough; the flaw is treating a necessary condition as a guarantee. Seven. No one has demonstrated that the negotiations are making progress. Clearly they've failed. Appeal to ignorance. Eight. We either double the aid budget or admit we don't care about the region. False dilemma. Nine. The report's author once worked for the opposition party, so its conclusions can be dismissed. Ad hominem — check the data, not the résumé. Ten. This policy is the best option because no alternative is superior to it. Circular — best and no alternative superior are the same claim in two coats. Eleven. Embassies that host cultural events get better local press. Ours should host more events to improve its press. Selection effect — embassies in friendly capitals may host more events and get better press; the comparison never isolates the cause. Correlation across units, causal advice extracted. Twelve. The program had forty complaints last year. It's clearly failing. Missing denominator — forty out of how many participants? Thirteen. After the reform, satisfaction is at seventy percent. The reform worked. Missing baseline — seventy up from what? If it was eighty before, the reform hurt. Fourteen. If sanctions are lifted, trade resumes. Sanctions were not lifted. So trade did not resume. Denying the antecedent — smuggling, third parties, exemptions; not-A never delivers not-B. Fifteen. The proposal should be rejected: it's long, its author is junior, and the font is unprofessional. Irrelevant criticism — three frowns, zero engagement with the argument. Now the second lap — five arguments, and this time the question varies, like the real section. Listen for what's being asked. Sixteen. All career diplomats speak at least two languages. Elena speaks four languages. What, if anything, follows about Elena? Nothing. Speaking languages is necessary for diplomats, not sufficient — Elena may be a linguist, a spy, or a barista. The premises never connect her to the diplomatic corps. Seventeen. The minister argued: our exports fell because the neighbor imposed tariffs. What must the minister be assuming? No alternative cause — that the fall isn't explained by recession, currency, or demand; negate it — suppose a recession did it — and her claim collapses. Eighteen. Post reports with more local staff produce faster consular services. Which fact would most strengthen the claim that local staffing causes the speed? A before-and-after with a control: posts that added local staff sped up, while similar posts that didn't stayed slow. Time order plus comparison. Nineteen. Either the cable was lost in transmission or it was never sent. The transmission logs are complete and show no loss. What follows? It was never sent — disjunctive syllogism, valid, granting the either-or is exhaustive. Twenty. 'The delegation will sign, because the foreign minister said the agreement serves her country's interests.' Which is the conclusion, and which the premise? Conclusion: the delegation will sign. Premise: the minister's statement. And the quiet assumption bridging them: that her stated view predicts the delegation's action. That's the gauntlet: twenty reps, every pattern in the vocabulary. If any name came slow, replay this drill — speed is the skill. Next: back to the world as it is now — great power competition, China, Russia, and the alliance lattice. Thanks for listening.
