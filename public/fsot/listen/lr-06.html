Transcript

Logic bootcamp two: inference — what must be true, and the discipline of knowing exactly as much as the passage says and not one syllable more. The reflex recap; out loud. Between two helpful assumption candidates? Take the weaker — necessary, not grand. Access is not? Accuracy. The inference standard, once, hard: an inference is compelled — if you can imagine any world where the premises hold and the candidate fails, it is not an inference. Today's traps: the tempting probable, the scope stretch, and the quantifier shuffle. Passage twice, question, silence, answer aloud. One. All section chiefs attended the briefing. Some attendees raised objections. Again: all section chiefs attended; some attendees objected. Can you conclude that some section chiefs objected? No. The objectors were attendees — but attendees may outnumber chiefs; perhaps every objector was a visitor. All chiefs are attendees puts chiefs inside attendees, and some attendees objected may land entirely outside the chiefs' circle. Draw the circles in your head; overlap is not compelled. Two. Some economists at the mission speak Mandarin. All the mission's Mandarin speakers have served in Beijing. Again: some economists speak Mandarin; all Mandarin speakers served in Beijing. What must be true? Some economists have served in Beijing — the some carries through the all: those Mandarin-speaking economists, whoever they are, all served. What must not be concluded: that all economists served, or that Beijing veterans all speak Mandarin — the all runs one direction only. This chain — some plus all yields some — is the licensed move; reversing an all is the forbidden one. Three. No officer who failed the language exam was posted to Vienna. Chen was posted to Vienna. Again: no exam-failer went to Vienna; Chen went to Vienna. What follows? Chen did not fail the exam — contrapositive, clean. But mark the boundary: did Chen pass? Not compelled — perhaps Chen never took it. Not failing and passing differ by exactly the case the test will hang an answer choice on. Precision about the complement: the opposite of failed is didn't fail, which includes never sat. Four. The budget rose every year from 2020 through 2024. In 2025 it did not rise. Again: rose every year 2020 through 2024; did not rise in 2025. Which is compelled: A, the budget fell in 2025; B, the 2025 budget was no larger than 2024's; C, the budget peaked in 2024? B. Did not rise means equal or lower — A insists on lower, too strong. C fails subtly: if 2025 equaled 2024, the budget is flat at its maximum — did it peak in 2024 or plateau through 2025? The passage can't say. Not rising is not falling; the space between is where wrong answers live. Five. Every cable the embassy sent this week was classified. The department received three unclassified cables from the region this week. Again: all this embassy's cables this week were classified; the department got three unclassified cables from the region. What follows? The three unclassified cables came from somewhere other than this embassy — another post, another agency. Not compelled: that the embassy sent few cables, or that the department received the embassy's cables at all. The inference is just the exclusion: unclassified cannot be from the all-classified source. Notice how modest that is; correct inferences usually feel almost disappointingly small. If an option feels informative and exciting, suspect it. Six. At least half the delegation speaks French. At least half the delegation speaks Arabic. Again: at least half speak French; at least half speak Arabic. What must be true? At least one member speaks both. Two halves of the same group cannot both avoid each other completely unless each is exactly half and they partition perfectly — but two at-least-halves must overlap in at least one person when the halves are strict, and even at exactly half-and-half, an odd-sized delegation forces overlap. The safe compelled claim: if either group exceeds half, overlap is forced; the test states it with sixty percent and seventy percent — then overlap of at least thirty percent is arithmetic, not judgment. Percent overlap: add them, subtract one hundred; that's the guaranteed minimum. Sixty plus seventy minus one hundred? Thirty percent, minimum, speak both. Seven, the discipline test — resist everything. The passage: the ministry announced that the pipeline's completion has been delayed to next spring, citing financing difficulties. Again: completion delayed to next spring; financing difficulties cited. Which is compelled: A, the pipeline will be completed next spring; B, financing difficulties caused the delay; C, the pipeline is not yet complete? Only C. A converts a schedule into a promise — delayed to spring doesn't guarantee spring. B converts a citation into a cause — governments cite; the real reason may be political. C is entailed: you cannot delay the completion of the completed. Announcements compel almost nothing except the announcement; what officials say is evidence of what they say. That reflex — read attribution as attribution — is worth points on the test and a career in the reporting cone. The residue, out loud. Some plus all yields? Some — the chain move. The opposite of failed? Didn't fail — including never took it. Did not rise means? Equal or lower. Overlap minimum? Add the percents, subtract one hundred. Cited means? Said — not proved. Next bootcamp: strengthen and weaken at full difficulty — competing hypotheses and the evidence that separates them. Thanks for listening.
