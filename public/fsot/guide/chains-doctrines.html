Chain: foreign policy doctrines

US Government · 3 min read

American foreign policy reads as a chain of named doctrines. Each answers the previous one.

Washington's Farewell (1796): no permanent alliances. Monroe (1823): Europe stays out of the Americas. Roosevelt Corollary (1904): the US polices the Americas. Wilsonianism (1918): self-determination and a League. FDR's Good Neighbor (1933): non-intervention in Latin America. Truman (1947): contain communism, starting with Greece and Turkey. Eisenhower (1957): resist communism in the Middle East. Kennedy: flexible response replaces massive retaliation. Nixon (1969): allies fight their own wars; détente. Carter (1980): the Persian Gulf is a vital interest. Reagan (1985): support anti-communist insurgents. Bush 41: new world order after the Gulf War. Clinton: enlargement of democracies. Bush 43 (2002): preemption. Obama: pivot to Asia, JCPOA. Trump: America First, tariffs, Abraham Accords. Biden: alliances rebuilt, Ukraine, Indo-Pacific.

Institutions run alongside: Rogers Act 1924 creates the Foreign Service; National Security Act 1947 creates the CIA, NSC, and DoD; NATO 1949; USAID 1961; War Powers 1973; Goldwater-Nichols 1986; DNI 2004.
