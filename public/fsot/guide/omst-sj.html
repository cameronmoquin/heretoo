OMST: situational judgment

OMST · 3 min read

Situational judgment items give you a workplace scene and ask for the best action. They are not personality questions and there is no credit for being agreeable. Every item is really asking: do you know who owns this decision, and do you know how fast it needs to move?

Run the same method every time. One, identify who owns the decision: you, your supervisor, a control officer, the security office. Two, name the category: security, ethics, and safety are hard rules, everything else is judgment. Three, eliminate any option that breaks a rule, no matter how sensible it sounds. Four, eliminate the options that do nothing, that wait, or that hand the problem to someone else without adding anything. Five, prefer the option that acts within your authority and tells the right person. Six, check timing, because for the same action, sooner almost always beats later.

Learn the wrong-answer archetypes and you can cut most items to two options on sight. The bulldozer acts far outside its authority. The ostrich waits, hopes, or says nothing. The escalator runs to the boss with something it could have handled. The people-pleaser bends a rule to be helpful. The tattletale reports a colleague before ever speaking to them.

Three rules override everything. Classified information and security procedures never bend for deadlines, seniority, or friendship. Mistakes are self-reported early, with an assessment of the damage. And routine friction with a colleague goes to the colleague first, not to the supervisor.

Watch for the qualifier in the stem. The best first step is not the same question as the best course of action: a first step can be a clarifying question, while a course of action usually has to resolve the matter. Read the stem twice; the whole item often turns on that one word.

Drill

Tap the six steps of the situational judgment method in order.

Show the full sequence
  1. Identify who owns the decision
  2. Name the category: security, ethics, safety, or judgment
  3. Eliminate options that break a rule
  4. Eliminate options that stall or pass the buck
  5. Prefer acting within authority and informing the right person
  6. Check timing: sooner beats later
