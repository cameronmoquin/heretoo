Chain: the amendments in runs

US Government · 2 min read

Memorize runs, not numbers. The numbers follow.

1 to 10, the Bill of Rights (1791). 11 and 12, early fixes: state immunity from suits (1798), separate ballots for president and vice president after the 1800 tie (1804). 13, 14, 15, Reconstruction: slavery, citizenship and equal protection, voting regardless of race (1865, 1868, 1870).

16 to 19, Progressive Era: income tax, direct election of senators, Prohibition, women's suffrage (1913, 1913, 1919, 1920). 20 to 22, FDR-era housekeeping: lame duck, repeal of Prohibition, two-term limit (1933, 1933, 1951). 23 to 26, the 1960s and 70s: DC electors, no poll tax, presidential disability and succession, vote at 18 (1961, 1964, 1967, 1971). 27, the stray: congressional pay raises wait for an election (proposed 1789, ratified 1992).

Trap pairs: 16 versus 17 (tax versus senators), 18 versus 21 (Prohibition on versus off), 24 versus 26 (poll tax versus age 18).

Memorize the sequence

Tap amendments 13 through 21 in order by subject.
