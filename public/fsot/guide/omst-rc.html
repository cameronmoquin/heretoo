OMST: reading comprehension

OMST · 2 min read

Reading comprehension on a timed test is not reading. It is evidence-matching against a short passage under a clock, and the winning habit is mechanical.

Work the method in order. One, read the question stem first, so you know what you are hunting. Two, read the passage for structure rather than detail: what is the claim, where does it turn, how does it end. Three, say the main idea in your own words before you look at any option. Four, go back to the text for the specific line that settles the question. Five, eliminate options that overstate, that add facts from outside the passage, or that answer a question you were not asked. Six, choose only what the passage can support, close to word for word.

The question types repeat. Main idea asks for the arc, not the most interesting sentence. Detail asks what the passage literally says. Inference asks what must follow from it, which is the logical-reasoning standard: must be true, not probably true. Vocabulary in context asks what the word does in this sentence, not its dictionary-first meaning. Author attitude asks for temperature, and the answer is usually milder than the extreme options offered.

The traps are consistent. An option is wrong if it is too strong, with words like all, never, or proves. It is wrong if it is true in the world but absent from the passage. It is wrong if it captures one detail and calls it the main idea. And it is wrong if it reverses a relationship the passage stated in one direction only.

Passages about workplace documents behave the same way as passages about history: find the claim, find the line, ignore what you know from outside. Your own expertise is the most common source of a wrong answer.
