The presidents, Washington to Buchanan

US History · 3 min read

Fifteen presidents before Lincoln, carried by one traditional mnemonic and its continuation. Learn the sentence first; the names hang off it.

The classic rhyme covers the first eight: Will A Jolly Man Make A Jolly Visitor? Will is Washington, A is Adams, Jolly is Jefferson, Man is Madison, Make is Monroe, A is Adams again (John Quincy), Jolly is Jackson, Visitor is Van Buren. One through eight.

The old long-form mnemonic carries you the rest of the way to Lincoln. Its next phrases run: Held The Poker, The Fiery Poker, But. Held is Harrison (William Henry, nine), The is Tyler (ten), Poker is Polk (eleven); The is Taylor (twelve), Fiery is Fillmore (thirteen), Poker is Pierce (fourteen), But is Buchanan (fifteen).

Tags for the first eight: Washington keeps out of Europe's wars; Adams signs the Alien and Sedition Acts; Jefferson buys Louisiana; Madison fights the War of 1812; Monroe issues the Doctrine; J.Q. Adams loses the 'corrupt bargain' rematch; Jackson kills the Bank and removes the Cherokee; Van Buren gets the Panic of 1837.

Tags for the last seven: William Henry Harrison dies in a month; Tyler annexes Texas; Polk takes the Mexican Cession and Oregon; Taylor also dies in office; Fillmore signs the 1850 Compromise; Pierce signs Kansas-Nebraska; Buchanan watches secession and does nothing. Everything here slopes toward Lincoln.
