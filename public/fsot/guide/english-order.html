English: ordering sentences

Math, Logic & English · 1 min read

Sentence-ordering items are solved by anchors, not by reading for meaning.

Find the sentence that can stand alone with no pronoun, no 'this', no 'then', no 'however'. That's usually first. Then chase antecedents: 'these reforms' needs reforms named before it; 'it' needs a noun; 'the debate' needs someone to have argued.

Transitions fix positions. 'For example' follows a general claim. 'As a result' follows a cause. 'Nevertheless' and 'even so' come after the thing they contrast, usually last. Ordinals are free points: first, second, then, finally.

For 'which sentence doesn't belong', the odd one changes subject, not tone.
