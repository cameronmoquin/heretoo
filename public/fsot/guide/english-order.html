English: ordering sentences

Math, Logic & English · 2 min read

Every one of these items is decided by backward reference. One sentence reaches back and points at something another sentence has to have introduced first. Find those pointers and the order assembles itself; read for general sense instead and you will find two sentences that both sound like openings.

The pointers are small and easy to read past. A demonstrative with a noun: this country, these reforms, these talks, such delays. A definite article on something not yet named: the initiative, the event, the agreement. A bare pronoun: it, they, their. And a proper noun used without explanation, when another sentence introduces it with a gloss — Connect 2022, an energy initiative launched by Colombia, is the introduction; Connect 2022 will create a business climate assumes you already met it.

Work backwards from each pointer. The event was broadcast so the programme could expand the reach of these talks: talks have to exist, so whichever sentence puts speakers in a room comes first, even though it reads like a detail. That one relation can settle an entire item.

Transitions confirm an order rather than establish one. For example follows a general claim. Additionally adds to something already added. Nevertheless contradicts the sentence before it. Use them to check the chain you built from pointers, not to build it.

Then read the options, which do more work than people expect. They are rarely four free permutations: often every option opens with the same sentence, which tells you the opening is not the question. Cross off any option that puts a pointer before the thing it points at, and you are usually down to two.

When two orders both survive, the tie-break is shape. Context before the thing that answers it; the general claim before its example; what something is before what it will do. A paragraph that opens by naming a problem and then introduces the programme addressing it is ordinary; one that describes the programme's effects before saying what it is, is not.

Drill

Four sentences from a post report. Tap them in the order that makes a paragraph.

Show the full sequence
  1. The consular section reopened its outreach programme in March after an eighteen-month suspension.
  2. The programme sends officers to regional universities to explain student visa requirements in person.
  3. These visits reached four campuses in the first quarter, two of them more than three hours from the capital.
  4. Attendance at the most distant campus was the highest of the four, which the section had not predicted.
