Chain: economic thought

Economics · 2 min read

Mercantilism (1500s to 1700s): wealth is gold; export more than you import; colonies exist to feed the mother country. Adam Smith, 1776, The Wealth of Nations: the invisible hand, specialization, attack on mercantilism. David Ricardo, 1817: comparative advantage; trade helps even the country worse at everything. Thomas Malthus, 1798: population outruns food. John Stuart Mill, mid-1800s: liberal political economy. Karl Marx, 1848 and 1867: class conflict, surplus value. Alfred Marshall, 1890: supply and demand curves, marginal analysis.

Keynes, 1936, The General Theory: in a slump, government spending restores demand; the multiplier. Hayek, 1944, The Road to Serfdom: central planning erodes liberty. Milton Friedman, 1960s: monetarism, inflation is monetary, permanent income. Samuelson: the textbook synthesis. Schumpeter: creative destruction. Sen: capabilities and the Human Development Index. Krugman: new trade theory.

Policy families: fiscal (Congress, taxes and spending, Keynes), monetary (the Fed, rates and money, Friedman), supply-side (tax cuts to spur production, Laffer, Reagan).
