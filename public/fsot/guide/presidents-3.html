The presidents, FDR to today

US History · 2 min read

The mnemonic's last stretch, then the ones inside living memory.

Rinse Those Empty Kettles Just Now, Friend. Rinse is Roosevelt (Franklin, thirty-two), Those is Truman (thirty-three), Empty is Eisenhower (thirty-four), Kettles is Kennedy (thirty-five), Just is Johnson (Lyndon, thirty-six), Now is Nixon (thirty-seven), Friend is Ford (thirty-eight).

No traditional mnemonic covers the last nine, because they are recent. Take them by initial instead: C-R-B-C-B-O-T-B-T. Carter, Reagan, Bush, Clinton, Bush, Obama, Trump, Biden, Trump.

Watch the second doubling. Trump is forty-five and forty-seven, with Biden forty-six between them: the same shape as Cleveland, and the only other time it has happened. Two split presidencies in the whole list, and both are counted twice.

Tags: FDR, New Deal and WWII; Truman, the bomb, the Doctrine, Korea; Eisenhower, interstates and Little Rock; Kennedy, Cuba; LBJ, civil rights and Vietnam; Nixon, China and Watergate; Ford, the pardon; Carter, Camp David and the hostages; Reagan, tax cuts and the Cold War's end; Bush 41, the Gulf War; Clinton, NAFTA and impeachment; Bush 43, 9/11 and Iraq; Obama, the ACA; Trump, tariffs; Biden, the Afghanistan withdrawal and the IRA.

Test trick: the adjacent-president swap. When two options are consecutive presidents, the question is testing exactly that boundary. Anchor the event to the president's signature issue, not to the decade.
