The plan to both tests

Two tests, five days apart.

Foundation

Weighted to the FSOT, because job knowledge is the largest body of material and it expires on the 14th. Work the history, government, economics and world affairs episodes and questions. Keep English going the whole way through: it is the one section both tests score. And run two situational judgment sessions a week even now, so the section you have never practised is not cold when you reach it.

FSOT rehearsal

Timed papers on the clock: Job Knowledge 30 in 30, Logical Reasoning 30 in 60, English 30 in 45. Review the wrong answers rather than taking more papers. Keep one situational judgment session a week ticking over, and refresh the current affairs episode in this window, since it is dated and the FSOT asks about the world as it is.

FSOT

Test day. Afterwards, put it down: the OMST is scored separately, and carrying one test into the next is how one bad morning becomes two.

Pure OMST

Five days, one job. Switch the app's question set to OMST only so sessions stop serving job knowledge. Work the nine OMST episodes, situational judgment and reading papers daily, and sit two full rehearsals at the real hour, late afternoon. Nothing new after the 17th.

OMST

Test day, five in the afternoon. One attempt in twelve months, so the preparation is finished before you walk in.

What carries over, and what does not

English expression is scored on both, so it is the best hour you can spend all month. Job knowledge — history, government, economics, world affairs, geography, math — is FSOT only and stops mattering on the 14th. Logical reasoning is FSOT only, but its habits transfer to OMST reading comprehension, because both turn on holding an inference to must be true. Situational judgment and reading comprehension are OMST only; situational judgment was cut from the FSOT in October 2025.

The three rules underneath it

Space it. Four half-hour sessions across four days beat one four-hour session, by a wide margin, for anything you need to still have a week later.

Interleave it. Mixing sections produces worse practice sessions and better test performance. Blocked practice feels more productive while you are doing it, which is exactly why people choose it.

Retrieve, don't reread. Answering out loud in the pauses is the whole point of the audio episodes. Listening without answering is roughly half the value.
